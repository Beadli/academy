# 0.5 Journal: your first entry

Every module ends with a journal entry, and this is the first one. In
Module 1 you'll get a proper engineering journal (a version-controlled
Obsidian vault), and this entry will migrate into it as its founding page.
For now, plain text in any editor is fine. Notepad is fine. Paper is
acceptable, though you'll be typing it up later.

Write four things, honestly, because nobody is grading this:

1. Why you're here. "Get a security job" is allowed. So is "my homelab is
   chaos and I want to understand what I built." Specific reasons survive
   the boring weeks better than vague ones.
2. Your machine: RAM, CPU, free disk. You'll measure these in the next
   lesson if you don't know them.
3. The tier you're starting on, from lesson 0.3.
4. Hours per week you can realistically give this. Write the number you
   can defend to yourself in a month, not the aspirational one.

Keep the file somewhere you won't lose it.

Why bother? Two reasons, one practical, one personal. Practical: by the
capstone, this journal becomes a portfolio, a written record of everything
you built and every problem you solved, in your own words, and it
interviews better than any certificate. Personal: six months from now
you'll want to remember why you started, and week-one you is the only
person who can tell you.
